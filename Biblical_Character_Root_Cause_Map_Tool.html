How to use this tool

Choose a trait, reflect on its possible roots, consider its corresponding virtue, and read the Scripture in context. These are prompts for self-examination, not a test, diagnosis or a way to judge another person's motives.

Feelings such as fear, anger or sadness are not automatically moral failures. Trauma, grief, illness and exhaustion may also influence behaviour. Forgiveness does not require abandoning wise boundaries.

Behind the scenes

1. Event

Something happens.

2. Interpretation

A belief or assumption gives it meaning.

3. Emotion

Fear, hurt, anger or longing may arise.

4. Choice

We can examine and redirect our response.

5. Pattern

Repeated responses shape habits.

Example: a colleague's promotion → “I'm being overlooked” → disappointment → either envy and resentment, or honest reflection, gratitude and wise action. These are possibilities, not inevitable chains.
